For each, state the wrong model in words a learner might actually use.
Explain briefly why that model can seem reasonable at first.
Give one diagnostic question that distinguishes it from the correct model.
Provide the expected reasoning and a minimal counterexample as a tutor-facing key.
Keep each counterexample within the learner's likely prerequisites.
Separate a harmless simplification from a false claim that breaks predictions.
Prioritize the misconception that blocks the next concept in the graph.
If switching into a live drill, ask only its diagnostic question first.
Withhold the key during the drill until the learner has answered.
